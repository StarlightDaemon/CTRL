Global VPN Infrastructure and Classless Inter-Domain Routing ArchitectureThe systematic identification and categorization of Virtual Private Network (VPN) infrastructure have become foundational requirements for contemporary network security, traffic engineering, and cybersecurity research. As VPN providers evolve from small-scale proxy services into massive, multi-national entities managing thousands of exit nodes, the underlying network architecture has grown increasingly complex. This complexity is characterized by a shift toward the acquisition of dedicated Autonomous System Numbers (ASNs), the utilization of diverse hosting partnerships, and the deployment of sophisticated obfuscation techniques designed to bypass geolocation and traffic analysis. An exhaustive analysis of major providers, including Mullvad VPN, ProtonVPN, NordVPN, ExpressVPN, Windscribe, and Private Internet Access (PIA), reveals a landscape where network addresses are not merely static identifiers but dynamic components of a broader privacy-preserving ecosystem.Architectural Paradigms of Modern VPN NetworksThe distribution of IP addresses within a VPN's network architecture is governed by several factors, including the ownership model of the hardware, relationships with upstream Internet Service Providers (ISPs), and the utilization of Anycast versus Unicast routing protocols. Most commercial VPN providers employ a hybrid infrastructure model, combining self-owned hardware in core data centers with rented dedicated servers from major hosting providers. This hybridity results in a complex web of Classless Inter-Domain Routing (CIDR) blocks that are often fragmented and subject to frequent rotation.The identification of these ranges typically relies on a combination of ASN analysis, public API scraping, and community-driven repositories. The az0/vpn_ip repository serves as a centralized hub for monitoring these shifts, providing scripts that resolve hostnames and aggregate IP addresses into usable lists for DNS-based or IP-based filtering.1 This repository is a critical resource for network administrators, security professionals, and researchers, offering unique lists of hostnames, Adguard-formatted blocklists, and raw IP addresses specifically for servers serving as VPN entry and exit points.1The Evolution of ASN Ownership and RoutingAn Autonomous System Number represents a collection of IP routing prefixes under the control of a single entity. For large VPN providers, obtaining their own ASNs allows for greater control over routing and a more consolidated IP footprint. However, even providers with their own ASNs often route a significant portion of their traffic through partner networks. This necessitates a tiered approach to identification: first, identifying the provider's primary ASN-controlled blocks; second, identifying dedicated blocks assigned to the provider within third-party ASNs; and third, identifying individual exit nodes that may be dynamically assigned from general-purpose hosting pools.The management of these addresses is not limited to simple blacklisting or whitelisting.
